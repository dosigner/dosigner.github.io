LEO Satellite–Ground FSOC

Drone-Based Terminal Emulator & RL Beam Control

A drone-based emulator of a LEO optical terminal, with wave-optics simulation and reinforcement-learning control of the uplink beam.

Problem

Time-varying atmospheric turbulence deflects and scintillates a ground-to-LEO laser link, while rapid satellite slewing makes static pointing loops fall behind.

Role

Led design and software prototyping of the drone-mounted LEO satellite terminal emulator. Built the uplink and downlink wave-optics simulation environment. First author of the LSTM-SAC beam-pointing control paper at SPIE (with Song, H.). Co-authored the technical demand survey and R&D proposal, which ranked 1st internally and was selected for the $5.9M DAPA Future Challenge.

Design judgment

Ground-to-LEO field trials cannot be repeated on demand under controlled turbulence. We split verification into two coupled tools: a wave-optics simulator to model phase screens and link budgets in software, and a hexacopter-mounted gimbal terminal to reproduce angular tracking kinematics in the field. Because atmospheric tilt evolves as a partially observable process (POMDP) under sensor delay, we paired a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) controller with an LSTM state encoder so the policy infers temporal turbulence trends from past observations.

Result

Published as first author at SPIE Optics + Photonics 2025 (DOI: 10.1117/12.3063817). The simulation and emulator framework formed the technical baseline for a $5.9M national defense R&D program.
